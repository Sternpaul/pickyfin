People

Say in a sentence or two what each person likes. That's what their picks follow most; watch history and Letterboxd add to it. Picks appear as rows in Pickyfin on the TV, and as playlists in every other Jellyfin app.

Movies

Every movie in the library and how the AI described it. Open one to correct it; movies you edit are never re-tagged.

Series

Every series in the library and how the AI described it: the same tags as films, plus kinds of TV films don't have (sitcom, procedural…) and how it's built. Open one to correct it; series you edit are never re-tagged. Series get rows of their own (Tuning), and what people watch as series counts toward their taste in films.

Issues

Films and series whose tags may be wrong: the AI didn't know the title, the tags don't fit the genres Jellyfin has, or a second AI disagreed. Correct them by hand, look them up on the web and tag them again, or mark them as fine.

Libraries

Which libraries recommendations may come from. Titles in a switched-off library are never recommended and never sent to the AI. Watch history from every library still counts toward taste. Series are described like titles, get rows of their own (Tuning) and count toward taste. Switch a TV library off to leave its series out entirely.

AI

Every film goes through three steps: 1 · Describe (an AI describes it from what it knows), 2 · Check (a second AI reviews the tags) and 3 · Look up (a web search finds real text, and an AI describes the film again from everything: the web text, both opinions, Jellyfin's genres and TMDB's keywords). Optionally, 4 · Re-check runs step 3 again on every film now and then. Status shows how far each step is. Changes on this page save on their own.

Free only

Never a cent: only OpenRouter's free models are used, and every other source stops before its free allowance ends (each shows what it has used). Turn it off to allow paid models and use past the free limits.

Manual run

One run through the titles and steps you choose, with any model from any source, paid or free. Apart from "Free only" and the model orders below. It shows an estimate first, and stops at the spending limit you set. Status shows it while it runs.

Provider

Any of these works. OpenRouter reaches most models with a single key.

API key

Stored on this server only. Leave the field empty to keep the saved key.

NVIDIA key

Optional. NVIDIA hosts free models too (get a key). With a key, its models can sit in every model order next to OpenRouter's, in any mix. NVIDIA's free use is meant for trying things out, allows about 40 requests a minute, and NVIDIA keeps what is sent.

Model for picking

Reads what you wrote about each person and picks their "Recommended for you" row. It deserves a capable model; if it fails, the describing order below takes over.

Custom endpoint

Only for a self-hosted or proxy endpoint that speaks the provider's API.

1 · Describe

Model for describing

Runs once per title, 20 titles per call; a fast, cheap model is fine.

Model order

Tried from the top. Each model gets 3 tries (waiting a little between them when it's busy or failing), then the next one takes over. Turn off the ones that keep failing. The numbers count calls since the server last started.

Advanced: describe every film again

2 · Check

Second opinion

A different model reviews every title's tags, 40 at a time, and names only what looks wrong. Much quicker than describing. Flagged titles show up under Issues.

Model order

Same as for describing: from the top, 3 tries each. Best with different models than describing, so they catch different mistakes.

Advanced: check every film again

3 · Look up on the web

A web search finds real text about each film (Wikipedia, Letterboxd, reviews…), and an AI describes the film again from that text together with both earlier opinions.

Search engines

Tried from the top. Each lookup goes to the first engine that's on and still has allowance left (per day or per month, and a dollar limit for paid ones); when one fails or finds nothing, the next takes over. So "Exa 40 a day, then Linkup, then Parallel" uses Exa first each day until its 40 are used.

    Wikipedia

    The film's Wikipedia article (summary, plot, reception) added to every lookup, free and without a key. While it's on, the engines above are told to leave Wikipedia out, so their pages are other sources.

    Engines per lookup

    How many engines answer each lookup. More engines, more pages for the AI to judge by; each one counts against its own allowance.

    Look up flagged titles right away

    When the AI doesn't know a title, or something looks off, look it up and describe it again at once. Each title is looked up once.

    Look up the whole library

    Slowly works through every title, a few every few minutes: flagged titles first, then lesser-known ones. With the second opinion on, a title waits until it has been checked, so all three steps happen in order.

    films a day (0 = off)

    Model order for describing again

    By default the same order as step 1. Set it apart if you want a stronger model only for looked-up films; they're few, so even a paid model costs little here.

    4 · Re-check everything

    How often

    Every film goes through step 3 again: a fresh web lookup (new reviews, new consensus on Wikipedia, Letterboxd, IMDb…), then the step-3 model describes it again from everything collected. It keeps tags that still fit and changes only what's clearly wrong. Spread evenly over the time you choose, so each pass ends as the next begins. Films you edited or marked as checked are left alone.

    Advanced: look up and describe every film again now

    Keywords and scores

    TMDB key

    Optional. Adds precise keywords from The Movie Database ("heist", "time travel"), the series a film belongs to (so parts come in order), and the films TMDB's viewers liked alongside it. A free key comes from themoviedb.org → Settings → API.

    OMDb key

    Optional. Real scores for every film: Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic and IMDb. They replace the guessed rating in the picks. A free key (1,000 requests a day) comes from omdbapi.com → API Key.

    OMDb requests per day

    One request per title. Titles are fetched again after six months, new titles after a month.

    Letterboxd

    Letterboxd ratings and reviews

    Optional. Pickyfin reads them from letterboxd-collector, a separate small service you run next to Pickyfin. It collects each film's Letterboxd rating and its most-liked reviews once a week. The rating counts most in the picks and shows on the film page and in the TV app; the reviews help matching by meaning and show on the film page. Pickyfin itself never contacts Letterboxd.

    How to set it up

    Add the collector (and FlareSolverr, which it needs for the reviews) to the same Docker stack as Pickyfin. Pickyfin then finds it on its own at http://letterboxd-collector:8098; nothing to fill in here.

      letterboxd-collector:
    image: ghcr.io/sternpaul/letterboxd-collector:latest
    container_name: letterboxd-collector
    restart: unless-stopped
    environment:
      JELLYFIN_URL: http://jellyfin:8096
      JELLYFIN_API_KEY: ""   # its own key: Dashboard -> API Keys
      LBXD_FLARESOLVERR_URL: http://flaresolverr:8191/v1
    volumes:
      - ./letterboxd-data:/data
  flaresolverr:
    image: ghcr.io/flaresolverr/flaresolverr:latest
    container_name: flaresolverr
    restart: unless-stopped

    The first run takes a while (about 10 seconds per film); Pickyfin picks up new ratings at its next check. Running it elsewhere, or behind a token? Fill in its address and token below. All options are in the collector's README.

    Collector address

    Empty = http://letterboxd-collector:8098, the collector in the same Docker stack. Otherwise for example http://192.168.1.20:8098.

    Collector token

    Only if the collector has API_TOKEN set. Type a single - to remove a saved token.

    Check for new ratings every

    How often Pickyfin reads the collector. The TV app downloads all ratings at start and then as often. The collector itself refreshes ratings once a week.

    hours

    Matching by meaning

    Match titles by meaning

    Turns each title's whole description (plot, tags, what the web says) and each person's description into a "meaning". Titles that mean similar things are related even without shared tags. Runs on your OpenRouter key, and on any of the sources below you add a key for; free models cost nothing. Explore the result on the Matrix page.

    More sources

    Each is optional. With a key, its embedding models appear in the model order below, next to OpenRouter's and NVIDIA's (NVIDIA's key is on the AI page). Each note says when it stays free. Keys are stored on this server only; leave a field empty to keep the saved key.

    Cloudflare get a token

    Free up to 10,000 "neurons" a day for the whole account (about 9 million tokens with BGE-M3); on the Workers Paid plan, more is billed. Pickyfin stops at 9,000 a day and goes on after 00:00 UTC, so it stays free either way. Doesn't keep what is sent. The token needs the "Workers AI" permission; the account ID is on the Workers AI page.

    Gemini get a key

    Free only while the key's Google project has no billing account: AI Studio's Projects page should say "Free" under Billing Tier. With billing linked, every request is charged. Free use allows about 1,000 requests a day. Outside the EU, UK and Switzerland, Google may use free-tier text to improve its products.

    Jina get a key

    10 million free tokens once (a few passes over a library); after that it stops unless you buy more. Its free models are for non-commercial use.

    Voyage get a key

    200 million free tokens once for each voyage-4 model. Without a card on file it allows only 3 requests a minute and simply stops when the free tokens are used up. With a card it's fast, the free tokens count first, and after them the card is charged.

    Mistral get a key

    Paid. Mistral's API has no free use; a Vibe Pro plan includes some API credit. Its models are used in manual runs, and in the normal runs only with "Free only" off. mistral-embed costs about $0.10 per million tokens.

    Model order

    Every model in the order matches all titles and builds its own matrix (meanings from different models can't be mixed, so each keeps its own). How close two titles are is the average over the models that know both, which is steadier than any one model. A busy model is tried three times, then skipped for that round while the others carry on; a model OpenRouter no longer offers drops out. More models means more requests, so the first ones fill up first.

    Tuning

    How picks are chosen, for everyone. Changes save on their own, and everyone gets fresh picks shortly after.

    What gets picked

    Description or watch history

    When both exist, which one the picks follow more. On shared TVs, the description is usually the better guide.

    Watch historyDescription

    Tags or meaning

    How titles are compared with each other and with a description: by their tags (subcategories, moods, themes…), or by what their whole description means. Only matters once titles are matched by meaning (AI page). Checked against TMDB's "viewers also liked" and real Letterboxd ratings, 70% meaning ranked people's favourites highest; tags still decide what someone wants to avoid.

    TagsMeaning

    Safe picks or discoveries

    Well-known, highly rated titles, or lesser-known ones they probably haven't heard of. Discoveries still need decent ratings.

    Safe picksDiscoveries

    Recommend series

    Series get rows of their own: "Series for you" and "Because You Watched" a series, from series the person hasn't started (started ones are Jellyfin's "Next Up"). The rows are made and shown on each person's page; the TV app doesn't show them yet. Off: series only count toward taste.

    How much series count for films

    What someone watches as series tells about their taste in films too: a person who finished three seasons of a slow Nordic crime show likely enjoys such films. Series they gave up on after an episode or two don't count, nor do reality, talk and game shows.

    Not at allLike films

    How much films count for series

    The other way round: the films someone watched shape which series they're recommended. Their own series always count fully there.

    Not at allLike series

    Variety

    How many titles of the same kind may appear together. "Little" stays close to what they love.

    New arrivals

    Push titles added in the last 30 days, so new additions get noticed.

    Change between refreshes

    How strongly last time's picks move down, so rows don't show the same titles day after day.

    Skip weakly rated titles

    Leave out titles with a low rating from real scores (OMDb, the web, Jellyfin). Titles without one are kept.

    Rows

    "Because You Watched …"

    Rows built around the latest films the person finished (and, with series rows on, the latest series they watched, for series rows). "Recommended for you" is always there.

    Movies per row

    Around twenty fills a TV row without padding.

    Fresh picks at least every

    Also right after someone finishes a film, and whenever you change what you wrote about them.

    "Because You Watched …" rows

    These only change the two "Because You Watched" rows; "Recommended for you" isn't affected. The middle is the default; turned up too far, the row fills with films that share an actor or a country but not much else.

    Matrix

    Every title on one map: the closer two titles sit, the more alike they are. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to move around, and click a title to see what it's closest to. Series have their own matrix (switch at the top of the map): titles are only ranked against titles and series against series, so series never shift a title's scores. "Both" puts titles (dots) and series (rings) on one map, and a picked title shows its closest titles and series.
